# Response R07

**The JSON does not identify which atoms drove ToxPred’s call.** It gives dofetilide a hERG blocker score of 0.659, above the model’s 0.413 threshold, but includes no atom attribution or analogue comparisons. Its applicability check only confirms that the elements are familiar to the training data.

A plausible structural explanation is the **basic tertiary amine linked to two aromatic arms bearing methanesulfonamide groups**, with the spacing and flexibility between them. Experiments on dofetilide analogues show that changing these features can change hERG affinity. That supports them as features to investigate, but does not establish what this particular model used. [Dofetilide analogue study](https://chemistry-europe.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/cmdc.200900203)

**The prediction also does not reveal the blocking mechanism.** Separate experiments support dofetilide binding in the channel’s inner pore, with pore residues including Tyr652 and Phe656 affecting block. They do not justify assigning a specific interaction between the protonated amine and those residues; one direct test found no evidence that cation–π interactions at either residue explain dofetilide block. [Pore mutagenesis study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9486667/); [interaction study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5762913/)

To identify the *model’s* drivers, I’d compare its predictions for matched changes to the amine, rings, sulfonamides, and linkers, then check the resulting hypotheses with hERG electrophysiology.
