## Short answer

Yes — "tens of micromolar" is enough to matter here, and moxifloxacin is the canonical proof of that. It's the ICH E14 positive control in thorough-QT studies precisely because it reliably produces a ~10–14 ms ΔΔQTcF at therapeutic exposure. So a double-digit-µM hERG IC₅₀ should not be read as reassuring on its own; what matters is the ratio to *free* plasma concentration.

**Rough margin arithmetic (do this before anything else):**

| Quantity | Approximate value |
|---|---|
| Total Cmax, 400 mg PO | ~3–4.5 mg/L → ~8–11 µM (MW 401) |
| Plasma protein binding | ~40% → free Cmax ~5–7 µM |
| Reported hERG IC₅₀ | ~30 µM (37 °C) up to >100 µM (RT protocols) |
| **Free-Cmax margin** | **~5–10×** |

Redfern's heuristic asks for ≥30× hERG IC₅₀ over free Cmax for comfort. A 5–10× margin lands moxifloxacin squarely in the "expect a measurable QTc signal" zone — which is exactly what's observed clinically. The wide IC₅₀ spread in the literature is itself the tell: room-temperature, low-frequency, non-steady-state protocols systematically *under*estimate potency, sometimes by 3–5×.

One important distinction: moxifloxacin prolongs QT but has a low observed TdP incidence. QTc prolongation is a biomarker, not the endpoint. Under CiPA it sits in the intermediate-risk category — relatively selective hERG block with little counterbalancing ICaL/late-INa block, which is the unfavourable pattern, but modest absolute potency keeps net risk low.

## On the ToxPred output

The `non_blocker` call (p = 0.14) is **not inconsistent** with what's known, but it also can't answer your question. These classifiers are trained on thresholded labels (typically IC₅₀ ≤10 µM = blocker), so "non-blocker" means "probably not sub-10-µM," not "no QT liability." It returns no potency and therefore no margin — and margin is the entire question. Don't let it substitute for the arithmetic above.

I'd also treat the Tox21 flags with scepticism for this chemotype. NR-AR-LBD (0.69), PPARγ (0.60), SR-ARE (0.53 vs 0.50 threshold), SR-p53 (0.49 vs 0.35 threshold) are all near or barely over their cut-offs, and there's no mechanistic or clinical basis for nuclear-receptor activity in a zwitterionic fluoroquinolone. Note also NR-AR: p = 0.79 called *inactive* because the threshold is 0.94 — the per-assay thresholds are asymmetrically calibrated, so the boolean labels carry much less information than they appear to. And the applicability check only confirms element composition, not structural similarity to training space.

## What would actually settle it

**Potency, done properly**
- hERG patch clamp at 37 °C, physiological K⁺, with a ventricular AP-shaped or step-ramp protocol, to true steady state (moxifloxacin equilibrates slowly); report IC₅₀ with CIs and Hill slope.
- Test frequency/use dependence and check for channel trapping — both shift effective potency at physiological rates.
- Chronic (24–48 h) hERG trafficking assay; acute block and trafficking inhibition are dissociable liabilities.

**Exposure, done properly**
- Species-matched plasma protein binding by ultrafiltration or RED, to convert IC₅₀ into a defensible free-Cmax margin.
- Worst-case exposure scenarios: renal impairment, hepatic impairment, DDI-driven increases, supratherapeutic dosing.
- Metabolites: moxifloxacin's sulfate and glucuronide conjugates are considered inactive, but confirm neither reaches channel-relevant free concentrations.

**Mechanism-level integration (CiPA)**
- Multi-channel panel: Nav1.5 peak and late, Cav1.2, Kv4.3/Ito, KvLQT1/IKs, Kir2.1/IK1.
- Feed into the ORd-CiPA in silico model for a qNet / torsade-metric score — this is what separates "prolongs QT" from "proarrhythmic."

**Functional confirmation**
- hiPSC-CM MEA or optical voltage assay: FPD/APD prolongation, triangulation, EADs, arrhythmic beats across 1–30× free Cmax.
- If needed, rabbit ventricular wedge or Langendorff for repolarisation reserve and TdP propensity under low-K⁺/bradycardia challenge.

**In vivo / clinical**
- Telemetered dog QTc with time-matched PK for a concentration–QTc slope.
- For moxifloxacin itself this already exists: robust exposure–response data with a slope of roughly 3 ms per mg/L, which is the definitive answer and the reason it serves as the assay-sensitivity control.

## Practical framing

If moxifloxacin is a *comparator* in your program, the useful move is to treat it as your calibration point: generate its hERG IC₅₀ and hiPSC-CM response in *your* assay alongside your compounds, and benchmark margins against it. A compound with a 5× free-Cmax margin behaves like moxifloxacin — measurable QT, manageable risk in a serious indication; 30×+ gives you real comfort.

If this is a new fluoroquinolone analogue, note that hERG activity in this series tracks strongly with the C7 amine basicity and lipophilicity. The diazabicyclononane here is a reasonably favourable starting point, but that SAR is where you'd design against it — and it's exactly what a binary classifier can't guide you on.
