# Response R03

**hERG is unlikely to block development of fexofenadine at exposures comparable to its approved use.** ToxPred calls it a non-blocker (0.251, below its 0.413 cutoff), but that score is a screening result, not a measured hERG potency or safety margin; its applicability check also did not assess structural similarity to the training set.

The experimental and clinical evidence is more persuasive. In one electrophysiology study, fexofenadine did not inhibit hERG current at 100 µM, whereas its parent, terfenadine, blocked the channel. The [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2006/021963lbl.pdf) reports no significant increase in mean QTc in 714 subjects given up to 240 mg twice daily, or in 40 volunteers given up to 400 mg twice daily. These findings support a **low hERG risk**, rather than a claim of zero risk at every possible exposure. [Electrophysiology study](https://bpspubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1038/sj.bjp.0704873)

**Next study:** If this program needs its own cardiac safety result, run a concentration–response human hERG patch-clamp assay at 35–37 °C, verify the concentrations in the assay chamber, and compare measured inhibition with the highest plausible *unbound* clinical exposure. That directly tests the remaining exposure-margin question and follows [ICH E14/S7B guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/guidance/sites/default/files/hhs-guidance-documents/FDA/48737916fnl_Jan-2025-update.pdf). For unchanged fexofenadine at established doses, I would not put development on hold while obtaining it.
