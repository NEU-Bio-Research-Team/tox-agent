# Response R03

Yes. **The hERG block is weaker than for many high-risk compounds, but the therapeutic exposure margin is modest enough to matter.** After 400 mg daily, mean total peak plasma concentration is about 4.5 mg/L (≈11 µM); with 30–50% protein binding, estimated free peak concentration is about 5.6–7.8 µM. Published hERG IC₅₀ values vary with the patch-clamp protocol, including 29–65 µM in one study. That puts those IC₅₀ values only about 4–12 times above free peak exposure. [Prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=bd0c0690-ba48-41c5-b76b-b1a53c36f424&type=display); [patch-clamp study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1760709/).

The clinical evidence shows the consequence: at the approved dose, mean QTc prolongation is about **6 ms after oral dosing**, and rare torsade de pointes cases have been reported, usually with other risk factors. Thus this is a real, generally modest QT liability at therapeutic exposure, with greater concern when exposure rises or repolarization reserve is reduced. ToxPred’s “non_blocker” label should not override the measured block or clinical QT findings; its probability is a classification output, not an exposure-adjusted safety margin. [Prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=bd0c0690-ba48-41c5-b76b-b1a53c36f424&type=display).

For a discovery compound, the deciding package would be a **reliable hERG concentration–response curve at physiological temperature**, measured unbound peak exposure at the highest plausible clinical exposure (including relevant metabolites), and paired concentration–QTc data. Those establish whether the in vitro margin translates into a clinically meaningful QT effect; an IC₅₀ alone cannot establish torsade risk. [ICH E14/S7B guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/e14-and-s7b-clinical-and-nonclinical-evaluation-qtqtc-interval-prolongation-and-proarrhythmic).
