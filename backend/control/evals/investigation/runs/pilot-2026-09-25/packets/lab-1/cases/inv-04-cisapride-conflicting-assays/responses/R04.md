# Response R04

**Go with the patch-clamp data.** Cisapride is, in fact, the canonical case where binding assays under-call hERG risk — it's a CiPA reference "high-risk" compound and was withdrawn in 2000 for torsades.

## Why the binding assay is the unreliable readout here

**1. State dependence is the big one.** Cisapride binds the inner cavity of Kv11.1 (Y652/F656 aromatic cage) and strongly prefers the open/inactivated state. In a membrane-prep radioligand assay the channels are unclamped and predominantly closed — the drug can't reach or stabilize its site efficiently. Classic open-state blockers therefore show apparent Kd orders of magnitude weaker than functional IC50. You'll see the same artifact with terfenadine and some methanesulfonanilides.

**2. It's a competition assay against a specific probe.** [³H]-dofetilide or [³H]-astemizole only report on ligands whose footprint overlaps the probe's *and* that can compete under those conditions. Partial overlap, allosteric block, or slow on-rates (cisapride equilibrates slowly) all give false negatives in a fixed-timepoint displacement format.

**3. Free-concentration problems.** Cisapride is a lipophilic base (cLogP ~3.6, piperidine pKa ~8.5). In membrane preps it partitions heavily into lipid and adsorbs to plastic, so nominal ≠ free. Nonspecific binding of >90% is entirely plausible, which right-shifts the apparent curve.

**4. Binding ≠ function.** Even a correct Kd doesn't tell you fractional current block at physiological voltage/temperature — and QT prolongation is a functional phenomenon.

## Practical framing

- Reported manual patch IC50s for cisapride cluster around **6–45 nM** (protocol/temperature dependent — 37 °C and physiological step protocols give lower values than RT).
- Free therapeutic Cmax is roughly **2–3 nM**.
- That's a **safety margin of ~5–15×**, well under the ~30× (and preferably ≥100×) you'd want. This is a red flag regardless of the binding number.

## What I'd actually do

1. **Treat the binding screen as a prescreen with known false-negative liability** — useful for rank-ordering within a series, not for go/no-go. Don't let it retire a hERG flag.
2. **Confirm functionally**, ideally automated patch (SyncroPatch/Qube) at 37 °C with a physiological or CiPA-style voltage protocol, plus a step-ramp to capture use dependence. Include cisapride and a weak/negative control on the same plate to validate your system.
3. **Measure free fraction** in the assay buffer or use a low-adsorption format; report IC50 on free concentration.
4. Note cisapride also has a **trafficking/maturation liability** for hERG at sub-micromolar concentrations on chronic exposure — a second mechanism binding assays are blind to entirely.
5. Longer term, don't stop at hERG: multi-ion-channel (Nav1.5 late, Cav1.2) plus in silico action-potential modeling is what regulators now lean on under the revised **ICH S7B/E14 Q&As**, and single-channel hERG data alone tends to over- or under-predict proarrhythmia.

**Bottom line:** the discordance isn't a contradiction to be split — it's a well-characterized limitation of the binding format for state-dependent, lipophilic basic blockers. Cisapride is genuinely a potent hERG blocker; if your chemical series resembles it (basic amine + lipophilic aryl, flexible linker), assume the risk is real and design against it.
