# Response R09

**Go with the patch-clamp result.** For hERG, the functional electrophysiology measurement is the decision-grade assay; binding displacement is a screening surrogate that is well known to under-report potency for exactly this chemotype.

## Why the binding assay is likely the misleading one here

1. **State dependence.** Cisapride is an open/inactivated-state blocker that reaches the inner cavity (Y652/F656 aromatic cage) through the open pore. In a membrane-prep radioligand assay the channels are not cycling — the pore is largely closed and the drug has poor access to its site. Patch clamp with a depolarizing protocol measures the conformation the drug actually engages *in vivo*.
2. **Probe-site mismatch.** Standard tracers ([³H]dofetilide, [³H]astemizole) label overlapping but non-identical footprints. Partial or allosteric overlap produces shallow, incomplete displacement curves that read out as "weak" even when functional block is nanomolar.
3. **Assay artifacts that bias toward false negatives.** Cisapride is lipophilic and a weak base — nonspecific membrane partitioning and plastic adsorption lower free concentration; lysosomal/vesicular trapping in intact-cell binding formats does the same.

Empirically, cisapride's patch-clamp IC₅₀ sits in the **low tens of nanomolar** against I_Kr, and it is the textbook case of a drug withdrawn for QT prolongation/torsades. Binding assays have historically been the ones that missed such compounds, not the reverse — this is a large part of why ICH S7B/E14 and the CiPA framework specify manual or high-quality automated patch clamp as the reference method.

## What I'd actually do

- **Treat the compound as a potent hERG blocker.** For a molecule intended to progress, that means a real safety margin exercise, not a re-litigation of the binding data.
- **Confirm in your own hands** with manual or high-quality automated patch clamp at physiological temperature (33–37 °C), with solubility/adsorption controls and a verified free-concentration measurement. Room-temperature APC can shift IC₅₀ several-fold.
- **Compute the margin** as free-plasma-Cmax vs. hERG IC₅₀ — the conventional comfort zone is ≳30×, and cisapride fails this badly.
- **Use the binding assay only for what it's good at**: cheap rank-ordering within a close analog series where the binding mode is consistent. Do not use it to clear a compound.
- **Reconcile rather than discard.** If your colleague's displacement curve was genuinely shallow/incomplete, that is itself informative — it may indicate the tracer doesn't report the binding mode for this series, which would invalidate the assay for the whole program, not just this compound.

## On the ToxPred output

The model calls "blocker" at **p = 0.536 against a 0.413 threshold** — barely over the line, i.e., low-confidence. Since cisapride is a known potent blocker (and almost certainly in or near the training data), this is effectively a **calibrated under-call**, and it's a useful warning: for this benzamide/aryloxypropyl-piperidine series the model's probabilities are compressed near the decision boundary, so don't read a sub-threshold score in an analog as reassurance. The applicability block also only confirms element-level coverage (`similarity_domain: null`), so there's no real domain-of-applicability support behind the number.

The Tox21 flags (SR-ATAD5 0.534 vs 0.480; SR-p53 0.516 vs 0.350) are likewise marginal and, given the low thresholds, I'd treat them as weak hypotheses at best — not something to act on ahead of the hERG finding.

**Bottom line:** functional > binding for ion channels, and in this specific case the patch-clamp data align with the clinical record. The binding screen is the result that needs explaining, not the one that should drive the decision.
