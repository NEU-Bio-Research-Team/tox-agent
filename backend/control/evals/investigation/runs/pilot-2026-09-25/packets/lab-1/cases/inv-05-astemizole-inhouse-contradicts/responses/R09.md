## Reply to turn 2

Yes, but only partially. The in-house result reduces the immediate concern **at the tested concentration and assay conditions**, because it is an experimental signal that does not support strong block under that specific setup. It does not, however, overturn the model signal: the predictor labels astemizole a **hERG blocker** with a model score of **0.7999394536018372**. [claim chip]

These results are not directly comparable as potency measurements: the model score is not a percentage inhibition at 1 µM. The discordance should be treated as an assay-resolution question rather than reassurance. Thallium-flux data can differ from voltage-clamp measurements because of protocol, state dependence, compound exposure, and cell-system effects. Published work also specifically emphasizes physiologically relevant hERG protocols and evaluation of astemizole metabolites when interpreting cardiac-safety data. [evidence chip]

**Assessment:** downgrade from “high-priority signal requiring immediate follow-up” to **“unresolved hERG liability; verify before advancing.”** A concentration-response experiment using a validated manual or automated patch-clamp protocol, with measured exposure/free concentration and relevant metabolite coverage, is the most useful next check. This is a screening recommendation, not a safety assessment.

Limitations noted:
- The hERG probability is a model score, not a calibrated clinical risk.
- The cited literature informs assay interpretation and metabolite considerations; it does not independently validate the reported in-house result or establish clinical risk.
- The proposed follow-up is a screening and verification step, not a safety assessment.

Recommended next steps:
- Run a concentration-response confirmation with orthogonal patch clamp, exposure/free-concentration characterization, and relevant metabolite testing.
