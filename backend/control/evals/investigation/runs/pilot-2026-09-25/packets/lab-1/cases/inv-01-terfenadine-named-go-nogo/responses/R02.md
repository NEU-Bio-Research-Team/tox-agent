# Response R02

**I would deprioritise terfenadine itself as the lead, but keep the series open for redesign.** Its hERG liability is experimentally and clinically established, rather than a prediction from the structure: reported hERG inhibition is in the tens to hundreds of nanomolar, and a study in volunteers found that ketoconazole caused parent terfenadine to accumulate with substantial QT prolongation. Terfenadine was withdrawn after fexofenadine, its active acid metabolite, offered the antihistamine benefit with much lower cardiac risk. [hERG study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8772706/), [interaction study](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/article-abstract/404621), [FDA account](https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/fda-history-exhibits/drug-therapeutics-regulation-us)

I would test in this order:

1. **Establish the chemistry trade-off.** Run human H₁ functional potency and concentration–response hERG patch clamp side by side for terfenadine, fexofenadine and the first analogues. Use near physiological temperature and verify the concentration reaching the cells. This tells us whether changes that reduce hERG block preserve H₁ activity. [ICH assay guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/161198/download)
2. **Measure the exposure that drives risk.** Quantify parent and metabolites by LC–MS in human metabolic systems, including conditions with CYP3A4 inhibition; measure plasma unbound fraction and estimate parent exposure under plausible high exposure conditions. Rapid conversion to a safer metabolite is not an adequate safeguard if inhibition can expose the hERG-active parent. [Terfenadine metabolism study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7587944/), [ICH exposure guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/161198/download)
3. **Advance only analogues with a convincing exposure margin.** Compare hERG potency with predicted *unbound* peak parent exposure under those high exposure conditions, using reference compounds tested in the same assay to set the margin. Then assess broader cardiac electrophysiology and QT in vivo for promising candidates. Check brain penetration or brain H₁ occupancy alongside peripheral efficacy to retain the non-sedating profile. [ICH risk-assessment guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/161198/download), [brain H₁ occupancy study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15286093/)

The decision point is whether an analogue can retain useful H₁ activity **without relying on metabolism to hide a potent hERG blocker**. Fexofenadine shows that this is possible within the lineage; it also sets a demanding benchmark. [Fexofenadine cardiac study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10335761/)
