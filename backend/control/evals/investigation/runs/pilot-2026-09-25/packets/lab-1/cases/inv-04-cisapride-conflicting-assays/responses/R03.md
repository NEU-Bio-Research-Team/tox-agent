# Response R03

**Let the patch-clamp result drive the hERG safety decision.** It measures the effect that matters here: loss of hERG potassium current. Independent cisapride studies report potent block in the nanomolar range, with the apparent IC₅₀ changing with the voltage protocol. Cisapride has also been associated with QT prolongation and torsades de pointes. [Rampe et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9395068/), [Mohammad et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9374794/)

Weak radioligand displacement does not clear that liability. Displacement measures competition with a particular tracer under binding-assay conditions; it does not directly measure current block. Cisapride’s block depends on channel activation and the recording protocol, so assay conditions can give different apparent potencies. [Mohammad et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9374794/), [ICH E14/S7B guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/161198/download)

For a development decision, treat cisapride as a **strong hERG risk signal**. If the decision concerns a related candidate, measure *that compound’s* hERG current block and compare its IC₅₀ with projected unbound exposure; the cisapride result alone cannot establish the candidate’s risk. [ICH E14/S7B guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/161198/download)
