# Response R08

For **hERG liability triage**, let the **patch-clamp result drive the decision**. It measures the functional endpoint that matters: suppression of hERG/Kv11.1 current. Weak radioligand displacement does not adequately exclude functional block, particularly when block is state-dependent. The published study specifically reports cisapride block in automated patch clamp and supports state-dependent binding involving the hERG pore region. [citation]

The stored predictor is directionally consistent: it classifies cisapride as a hERG blocker, with a model probability of 0.5364413857460022. This is a model score, not a calibrated clinical risk. The appropriate posture is therefore to treat the compound as requiring hERG liability follow-up, rather than allowing the weak binding result to de-risk it. This conclusion is limited to hERG screening and does not establish clinical safety or a regulatory outcome.

Limitations noted:
- The hERG probability is a model score, not a calibrated clinical risk.
- The cited publication supports the hERG patch-clamp and state-dependence interpretation, but does not validate the colleague's specific radioligand result.
- This is a hERG screening and development-posture recommendation, not a safety, clinical, or regulatory conclusion.

Recommended next steps:
- Use the patch-clamp finding as the controlling hERG signal and perform orthogonal follow-up under matched concentration, voltage, state, and exposure conditions before making a development decision.
