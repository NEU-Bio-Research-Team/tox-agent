# Response R02

A tens-of-micromolar patch-clamp effect is not, by itself, enough to determine therapeutic concern. The key comparison is the **free unbound cardiac exposure at the intended dose**, including peak concentration and active metabolites, against the concentration-response relationship for hERG block. Clinical QT/QTc data are also needed because hERG block is an electrophysiology signal, not a direct prediction of patient outcome.

For this structure, the fixed predictor returns an hERG label of `non_blocker` with a model score of 0.14254340529441833. This is supportive context, not a substitute for exposure-matched electrophysiology or clinical ECG evidence.

What would settle the question:
- Measure unbound plasma exposure at therapeutic and supratherapeutic dosing, with metabolite coverage and relevant protein binding.
- Reproduce hERG concentration-response data under a validated protocol, reporting IC50, maximal block, temperature, voltage protocol, and kinetics.
- Integrate hERG with other cardiac currents and human cardiomyocyte action-potential assays.
- Conduct exposure-response QTc analysis, ideally a thorough-QT design, including the high-exposure scenario and correction for heart rate.
- Assess patient-specific modifiers such as electrolyte abnormalities, renal impairment, bradycardia, and concomitant QT-prolonging drugs.

This supports a targeted cardiac-safety verification plan, not a safety or clinical-use conclusion.

Limitations noted:
- The hERG probability is a model score, not a calibrated clinical risk.
- The proposed experiments are verification steps, not a safety or clinical-use assessment.
- The evidence search did not yield a usable compound-specific accepted record establishing moxifloxacin therapeutic exposure margin or QTc translation.

Recommended next steps:
- Prioritize an exposure-matched cardiac safety package: free Cmax and metabolite characterization, validated hERG concentration-response testing, and QTc exposure-response analysis.
