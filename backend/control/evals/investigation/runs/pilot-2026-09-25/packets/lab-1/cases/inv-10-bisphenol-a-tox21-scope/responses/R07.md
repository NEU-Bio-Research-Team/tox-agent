# Response R07

**No single “toxic overall” conclusion follows from this output.** ToxPred predicts *activity in specific Tox21 assays*, not the probability that BPA will cause harm at a given dose. Tox21 uses these assays to identify biological activity and prioritize follow-up testing; translating a hit into an adverse outcome requires exposure and other evidence. [NIEHS Tox21 overview](https://www.niehs.nih.gov/research/programs/tox21), [analysis of Tox21 interpretation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9624462/)

Here, 10 of 12 assay scores exceed their **assay-specific thresholds**. The clearest signal is estrogen receptor activity (NR-ER 0.989; NR-ER-LBD 0.997), which is consistent with published experiments showing BPA can activate ERα. The aromatase and PPARγ calls raise other endocrine-pathway questions. The ARE, HSE, ATAD5 and p53 calls point to stress-response assays, while SR-MMP concerns mitochondrial membrane potential. **An ATAD5 or p53 hit does not, by itself, establish DNA damage or mutagenicity.** [BPA receptor study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23562765/), [NCATS assay descriptions](https://ncats.nih.gov/research/research-activities/Tox21/assays)

Read each number as a **model score for an assay-activity label**. It is neither potency nor an effect concentration, and 0.999 for SR-MMP does not mean a 99.9% chance of mitochondrial toxicity in vivo. The two negative androgen-receptor calls and the hERG *non-blocker* call (0.160 versus a 0.413 cutoff) are likewise limited to those predicted endpoints; published cell experiments have reported BPA androgen-receptor antagonism. The “applicability: ok” check only confirms familiar elements—the report provides no structural-similarity assessment. [BPA receptor study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23562765/)

For a discovery decision, I’d treat this as a **substantial follow-up signal**, led by ER activity. Check measured concentration–response data for BPA, confirm priority hits with orthogonal assays, compare active concentrations with cytotoxicity and plausible *free* exposure, and check whether BPA was in the model’s training data. That will be more informative than counting positive labels or ranking the scores. [Tox21 data tools](https://tox21.gov/data-and-tools/), [analysis of assay artifacts and exposure limits](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9624462/)
