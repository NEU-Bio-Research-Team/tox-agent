# Response R05

No. These results do **not** establish that bisphenol A is “toxic overall.” Tox21 assays are separate, mechanism-oriented activity screens; they do not combine into a toxicity or severity score, and each reported probability is an uncalibrated model score rather than a clinical risk.

For this molecule, the model classifies activity in `NR-AhR`, `NR-Aromatase`, `NR-ER`, `NR-ER-LBD`, `NR-PPAR-gamma`, `SR-ARE`, `SR-ATAD5`, `SR-HSE`, `SR-MMP`, and `SR-p53`; it classifies `NR-AR` and `NR-AR-LBD` as inactive. Read each result independently: it is a signal that the corresponding assay endpoint may be active, not proof of a biological mechanism, in-vivo toxicity, exposure-related harm, or disease risk.

The appropriate interpretation is therefore: BPA shows a broad set of **model-predicted assay activities that warrant endpoint-specific verification**, while this output alone cannot support an overall toxicity or safety conclusion. Any follow-up screening would remain a hazard-characterization step, not a safety assessment.

Limitations noted:
- The model's activity probabilities are not calibrated clinical or real-world risk probabilities.
- Any proposed endpoint verification is hazard characterization, not a safety assessment.

Recommended next steps:
- If a decision is required, verify the individual flagged endpoints with fit-for-purpose experimental and exposure-context evidence.
