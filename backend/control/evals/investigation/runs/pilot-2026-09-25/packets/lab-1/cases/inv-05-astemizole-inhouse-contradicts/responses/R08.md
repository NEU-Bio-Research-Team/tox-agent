# Response R08

## Reply to turn 1

**High concern for systemic repurposing.** Astemizole is a potent hERG blocker: mammalian patch clamp studies report IC₅₀ values in the low nanomolar range, and its major metabolite, desmethylastemizole, is similarly potent and persists for days. [hERG study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10376921/)

This is an observed clinical liability, not just an assay signal. QT prolongation and torsades de pointes were reported with astemizole, including a case involving the metabolite when parent drug was undetectable; the drug was withdrawn from the US market after fatal arrhythmias. Exposure can rise with interacting drugs such as ketoconazole. [Clinical report](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/S0735-1097%2896%2900352-X) · [FDA review](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2005/021605s000_MedR_P1.pdf) · [Interaction case](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9071844/)

I’d treat the screen hit as **a strong reason to deprioritize astemizole itself** unless the proposed use has a compelling benefit and a credible route to negligible cardiac exposure. If it remains of interest, the key comparison is active exposure versus hERG inhibition for **both parent and metabolite** at steady state; the screening concentration alone cannot establish a safe margin.

## Reply to turn 2

**No.** I’d flag the 20% result as a discrepancy to investigate, but I would keep the high hERG concern. Astemizole blocks hERG at about 1–2 nM in published patch-clamp studies, and one published thallium-flux assay measured an IC₅₀ of 56 ± 15 nM. Flux assays can shift apparent potency substantially, so a single point at 1 µM cannot establish a safety margin or outweigh the electrophysiology and clinical history. [Patch-clamp study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10376921/) · [Thallium-flux study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3079779/) · [Assay comparison](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20658944/)

I’d repeat a concentration–response curve with a fresh, independently sourced astemizole standard and check the reference blocker’s full inhibition range. Verify the compound’s identity and its concentration in the assay medium; review solubility, incubation time, and fluorescence interference. Then confirm **parent and desmethylastemizole by patch clamp** under a validated protocol. Until that resolves the discrepancy, I would not use the flux result to downgrade systemic cardiac risk. [Parent and metabolite patch-clamp data](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12719398/)
