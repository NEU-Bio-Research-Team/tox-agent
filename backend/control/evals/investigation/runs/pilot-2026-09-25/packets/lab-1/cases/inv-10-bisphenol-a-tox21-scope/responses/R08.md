# Reading the ToxPred Tox21 output for bisphenol A

**No, the model isn't saying BPA is "toxic overall."** Tox21 endpoints are 12 separate in vitro assays, each asking whether the compound perturbs one pathway readout in a cell or biochemical system at some concentration. They carry no information about dose, exposure, ADME, or in vivo outcome. The output is a pathway-activity profile, not a hazard verdict. The model has no "overall toxicity" output, and counting the positives doesn't produce one.

## Reading the individual calls

**Strong, mechanistically plausible signals**
- **NR-ER (0.99) and NR-ER-LBD (1.00):** These are far above their thresholds. They match BPA's well-established estrogen receptor agonism, which is the main reason it's regulated. This is the most interpretable part of the output.

**High probability but needs context**
- **SR-MMP (1.00), SR-ARE (0.97), SR-p53 (0.93), SR-HSE (0.88):** These are stress-response assays. Many phenols hit them at the upper end of the Tox21 concentration range, often alongside cytotoxicity. A positive here can mean nonspecific cell stress rather than a specific mechanism.
- **NR-Aromatase (0.95):** The aromatase assay is inhibition-mode, so cytotoxicity and luciferase interference can produce false actives. Treat it with the same caution.

**Marginal calls**
- **NR-AhR** (0.75 vs. threshold 0.68) and **SR-ATAD5** (0.62 vs. 0.48) are close to their cutoffs. I wouldn't put much weight on either without data.
- **NR-PPAR-gamma** (0.79 vs. 0.42) is clearly positive but has a lower threshold. Moderate confidence.

**Negatives**
- **NR-AR (0.26)** is called inactive, but its threshold is 0.94. The model essentially never calls AR active, so this negative is weak evidence. BPA is reported in the literature as an AR antagonist, and this agonist-mode assay wouldn't capture that anyway.
- **hERG non-blocker (0.16 vs. 0.41):** Reasonably clear negative, consistent with expectations for a small, neutral bisphenol.

## Caveats about the model itself

1. **BPA is almost certainly in the training data.** It's one of the most tested compounds in Tox21. These predictions largely reflect recall of known labels, not a test of how well the model generalizes. Don't use BPA to judge how the model will perform on your novel compounds.
2. **The applicability check is minimal.** `element_rules_v1` only confirms that C, H, and O are common elements. It says nothing about structural similarity to the training set, and `similarity_domain` is null.
3. **Probabilities aren't potencies.** A 0.99 means the model is confident the compound was active in the assay. It doesn't mean the compound is potent. The Tox21 actives for BPA's stress-response assays are mostly in the tens-of-µM range.

## Practical takeaway

If BPA is a reference compound, the output reproduces its known ER liability, which is a reasonable sanity check. If you're assessing a bisphenol-like scaffold in your own series, I'd:

- Pull the actual Tox21/ToxCast concentration-response data (AC50s and cytotoxicity bursts) for BPA and close analogs from the EPA CompTox dashboard. That tells you which actives occur below the cytotoxicity range.
- Prioritize ER agonism as the real liability to design around, and confirm it with an orthogonal ER assay.
- Treat the stress-response and aromatase flags as "check cytotoxicity first" rather than as distinct mechanisms.
