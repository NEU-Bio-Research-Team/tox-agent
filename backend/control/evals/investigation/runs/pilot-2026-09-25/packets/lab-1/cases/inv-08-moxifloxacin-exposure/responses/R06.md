# Response R06

Yes. The hERG result is a concern at therapeutic exposure, even though the IC₅₀ is in the tens of micromolar. With 400 mg daily, mean steady-state peak plasma concentration is about 4.5 mg/L, or 11 µM total; 30–50% protein binding puts the estimated **free peak near 6–8 µM**. Published hERG IC₅₀ values vary substantially with assay conditions, including about 29 µM at 35°C. That leaves only a several-fold margin in that assay. [Product label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=182ecd0f-714f-42f0-9b16-fb604cf15786), [patch-clamp study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1760709/).

The clinical evidence resolves the main question: **moxifloxacin does prolong QTc at the therapeutic 400 mg dose**. The label reports a mean increase of 6 ms near peak concentration, and controlled QT studies find a concentration-dependent effect. Torsades de pointes has been reported rarely, particularly with other proarrhythmic conditions. [Product label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=182ecd0f-714f-42f0-9b16-fb604cf15786), [pooled concentration–QTc analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21228407/).

For an early discovery decision, I’d want measured **unbound peak exposure at steady state**, including plausible high-exposure patients; a hERG concentration–response curve with verified bath concentrations at physiological temperature; and human ECGs paired with drug concentrations to estimate QTc change at those exposures. Moxifloxacin already has the last piece: its weak-looking hERG potency does not translate into an absence of clinical QT effect.
