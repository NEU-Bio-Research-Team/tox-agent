# Response R06

**Treat cisapride as a potent hERG blocker.** Patch clamp measures the loss of channel current that matters for this decision. Published studies report concentration-dependent block at about 6.5–45 nM; one found that block required channel activation and varied with voltage. That makes weak displacement in a single radioligand screen insufficient to clear the compound. [Patch-clamp study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9374794/), [protocol comparison](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9395068/).

I would check the binding assay’s tracer, concentration range, and controls, then confirm the functional result in our own patch-clamp assay if the program depends on it. The discrepancy has no established explanation from the information given: another study measured cisapride displacement in a hERG radioligand assay at roughly 100 nM. ToxPred’s *blocker* label supports prioritizing that follow-up, but its 0.536 score is not a measured potency or a substitute for the electrophysiology. [Binding and functional assay study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5055135/).
