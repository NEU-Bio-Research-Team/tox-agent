# Response R01

I would use the predictions to **prioritize experiments, not to clear or condemn bortezomib**. I haven’t seen the model’s scores, cutoffs, or training data, so I can’t assess those particular outputs.

For **hERG**, a predicted negative result would not clear cardiac risk, and a positive result would not establish direct hERG block. Bortezomib’s [prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1521d321-e724-4ffc-adad-34bf4f44fac7) reports cardiac failure and isolated QT prolongation, while stating that causality for the QT cases is unestablished. I’d check for measured, concentration dependent hERG patch clamp data and compare potency with relevant *unbound* exposure, alongside the clinical ECG evidence. [FDA guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/e14-and-s7b-clinical-and-nonclinical-evaluation-qtqtc-interval-prolongation-and-proarrhythmic) treats these as an integrated assessment.

For **Tox21**, interpret each predicted endpoint as an assay hypothesis, not an overall toxicity verdict. The assays measure pathway or cell responses under specific conditions. Bortezomib is a particularly good reason to inspect viability controls: in a [Tox21 FXR study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4894417/), its apparent antagonist activity was judged *inconclusive* because it occurred at or near cytotoxic concentrations.

Before using either prediction in a decision, I’d verify the exact stereochemical structure and assay endpoint, check whether bortezomib or duplicate records appeared in training, and review the model’s applicability domain and measured assay curves.
