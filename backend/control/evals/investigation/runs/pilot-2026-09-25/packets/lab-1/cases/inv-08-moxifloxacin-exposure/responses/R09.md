# Response R09

**Short answer: yes, it's a concern — and moxifloxacin is the textbook case that proves "tens of micromolar" is not reassuring on its own.** Moxifloxacin is the standard *positive control* in ICH E14 thorough QT studies precisely because a ~30 µM hERG IC50 produces a reproducible, clinically measurable QTc effect at label dose.

## Run the margin, not the potency

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| hERG IC50 (patch-clamp, literature) | ~30–130 µM depending on temperature/protocol (often cited ~29–50 µM at 35–37 °C) |
| Cmax, 400 mg PO | ~4.3 µg/mL ÷ MW 401.4 → **~11 µM total** |
| Plasma protein binding | ~30–50% → **free Cmax ~6–7 µM** |
| Free margin to hERG IC50 | **~4–10×** |
| Observed ΔΔQTcF at therapeutic dose | **~+10 to 14 ms** (C–QTc slope ~3–4 ms per µg/mL) |

That margin sits far below the ~30× free-Cmax hERG/exposure ratio that Redfern and subsequent analyses associate with a low-risk profile. So the *in vitro* number and the *in vivo* outcome are entirely consistent — the trap is comparing a micromolar IC50 against a nanomolar intuition without normalizing to free exposure.

Worth separating two endpoints, though: moxifloxacin reliably prolongs QT, but its clinical TdP rate is low. It behaves as a relatively "clean" IKr blocker — no substantial ICaL/late-INa block to balance it, but also no trapping, shallow slope, short treatment course, and no meaningful CYP-mediated accumulation (it's cleared by glucuronidation/sulfation). CiPA placed it in the **intermediate**-risk bin, not high risk like sotalol or dofetilide. For your compound, "QT signal" and "torsade risk" need to be answered separately.

## What would actually settle it

**Fix the exposure denominator first — it's usually the bigger source of error than the IC50:**
- Unbound Cmax at the highest intended dose, at steady state, using species-matched plasma protein binding (measured, not predicted)
- Worst-case scenarios: renal/hepatic impairment, DDIs, food effects, accumulation ratio
- Active/circulating metabolites — check whether any are hERG-active
- Cardiac tissue-to-plasma partitioning if you have any reason to suspect accumulation

**Firm up the hERG number:**
- Physiological temperature (37 °C), physiological K⁺, and a step-ramp or action-potential-clamp protocol — room-temperature IC50s are routinely right-shifted several-fold
- Confirm free concentration in the bath (LC-MS/MS of perfusate); lipophilic/adsorptive compounds lose large fractions to tubing and plates, which inflates apparent IC50
- Full concentration–response with Hill slope, plus onset/offset kinetics, use dependence, and trapping (state-dependent block)

**Then go beyond hERG — this is where risk is actually adjudicated now:**
- CiPA ion-channel panel: peak and late INa (Nav1.5), ICaL (Cav1.2), IKs, Ito, IK1 — a co-block of ICaL or late INa substantially changes the interpretation
- In silico ORd/CiPA simulation → qNet / torsade metric score against the 28-drug training set
- hiPSC-CM MEA or voltage-sensor assay: field potential duration, plus arrhythmia-like events (EADs, beat irregularity)
- Optional mechanistic triangulation: rabbit ventricular wedge or Purkinje fiber for TRIaD endpoints (triangulation, reverse use dependence, instability, dispersion)

**In vivo and clinical:**
- Conscious telemetered dog or NHP QTc with **concurrent PK**, so you get an exposure–response relationship rather than a dose–response one; design to reach multiples of projected clinical free Cmax
- In Phase I, collect intensive time-matched ECG + PK and build a **concentration–QTc model** (ICH E14 Q&A R3 permits this in lieu of a dedicated TQT). The deliverable is the upper 90% CI bound of ΔΔQTc at the clinical high exposure — <10 ms is the bar.

**Bottom line for triage:** compute free-Cmax margin and require ≥30× against a 37 °C, concentration-verified hERG IC50 for comfort. Moxifloxacin's ~5× margin is exactly why it makes a dependable positive control. If your candidate lands in that same range, plan a CiPA package and a well-powered C-QTc assessment in Phase I rather than treating the micromolar IC50 as a clean bill of health.
