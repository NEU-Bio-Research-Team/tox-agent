# Response R09

**Recommendation: hold prioritisation pending a cardiac-safety gate, rather than treating the model result alone as an automatic stop.**

The predictor flags terfenadine as a hERG blocker, with a model blocker probability of 0.680. This is consistent with compound-specific literature describing terfenadine-associated QT prolongation and torsades de pointes through IKr blockade. That combination is strong enough that I would not advance it as an unqualified lead.

The next test should be exposure-informed manual-patch-clamp hERG concentration-response testing for terfenadine and relevant metabolites, including fexofenadine, followed by an orthogonal cardiac repolarisation assay such as human iPSC-cardiomyocyte field-potential or action-potential testing. Metabolite physicochemical and exposure differences may change translation, so the parent compound result should not be interpreted in isolation.

If liability reproduces at relevant free concentrations and in the orthogonal assay, deprioritise it. If adequate exposure margins are demonstrated and repolarisation effects do not reproduce, reassess the hold decision. These are screening tests, not a clinical safety assessment.

Limitations noted:
- The hERG probability is a predictor score, not a calibrated clinical or programme-level risk.
- The external evidence includes a review and a computational metabolite study; it does not replace a current, exposure-matched experimental package for this programme.
- The proposed electrophysiology assays are screening and decision-support tests, not a clinical safety assessment.

Recommended next steps:
- Run exposure-informed manual-patch-clamp hERG concentration-response testing for terfenadine and relevant metabolites, followed by orthogonal cardiac repolarisation testing.
