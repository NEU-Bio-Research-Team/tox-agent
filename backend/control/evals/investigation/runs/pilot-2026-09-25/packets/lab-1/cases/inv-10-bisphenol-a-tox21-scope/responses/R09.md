# Response R09

No. These results do not mean bisphenol A is “toxic overall.” They indicate predicted activity in particular Tox21 assay systems.

**How to read them**
- The strongest signal is in the estrogen-receptor assays: NR-ER is classified active, while NR-AR is classified inactive. These are separate endpoint results, not a global toxicity label.
- Other active calls concern different biological systems, including AhR, aromatase, PPAR-gamma, oxidative-stress, DNA-damage, heat-shock, mitochondrial-membrane, and p53-related assays. They should be reviewed assay by assay; the number of active assays is not a severity score.
- Each probability is an uncalibrated model score. “Active” means the model score crossed that assay’s stored decision threshold; it does not establish potency, dose relevance, mechanism, or human risk.
- Applicability is marked “ok” only under an element-composition rule. That supports using the output as a screening signal, not proof that the compound is fully represented by the training distribution.

The practical conclusion is: treat this as a prioritization signal for endpoint-specific confirmation, not as an overall toxicity determination. A useful follow-up is concentration-response testing with controls and orthogonal confirmation for the highest-priority receptor and stress-response signals, interpreted against a defined exposure context.

Limitations noted:
- Model probabilities are not calibrated clinical or population risk estimates.
- Applicability is based on element rules and does not establish full distributional similarity.
- The proposed follow-up is screening and verification, not a safety assessment.

Recommended next steps:
- Confirm the highest-priority receptor and stress-response signals experimentally using concentration-response controls, orthogonal assays, and a defined exposure context.
