# Response R08

**Yes—deprioritise terfenadine itself as a development candidate.** ToxPred’s hERG score (0.68, above its 0.41 cutoff) is a screening flag, not an IC₅₀ or a probability of clinical arrhythmia. Here, though, the concern is established independently: terfenadine blocks hERG, and a clinical interaction study found that ketoconazole caused parent terfenadine to accumulate alongside marked QT prolongation. Terfenadine was withdrawn after serious cardiac events; its active acid metabolite, fexofenadine, retained antihistamine activity with a substantially better cardiac profile. [Channel study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8772706/) · [Interaction study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8445813/) · [FDA history](https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/fda-history-exhibits/drug-therapeutics-regulation-us)

I would keep terfenadine as a **reference scaffold and positive control**, and prioritise analogues only if they remove the parent’s cardiac liability while retaining H₁ activity. The next experiments, in order, are:

1. **Measure concentration–response hERG block** for terfenadine, fexofenadine and early analogues using the same patch clamp protocol, alongside H₁ functional potency.
2. **Establish exposure margins:** measure protein binding, solubility and parent/metabolite formation in human hepatocytes, including CYP3A4 inhibition. Compare hERG potency with projected *unbound* parent exposure under the highest plausible exposure condition. A nominal hERG IC₅₀ alone cannot answer the go/no-go question. [ICH E14/S7B guidance](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/E14-S7B_QAs_Step4_2022_0221.pdf)
3. For analogues with a credible margin, assess other cardiac ion channels and cardiomyocyte electrophysiology, then exposure-matched in vivo QT/ECG. Check brain penetration and sedation potential in parallel for the programme’s intended profile.

ToxPred’s three Tox21 positive calls are near their assay cutoffs; I would follow them up with concentration–response assays for promising analogues, but they do not change the immediate cardiac decision.
