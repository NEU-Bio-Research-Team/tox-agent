# Response R05

## Reply to turn 1

Treat astemizole as a **high-priority hERG follow-up signal** in the screen: the model predicts a blocker and assigns an approximately 80.0% blocker probability. This is an uncalibrated model score, not a clinical arrhythmia-risk estimate.

The applicability flag is `ok`, but it is only an element-rule check and does not confirm close distributional similarity. No accepted compound-specific hERG evidence was retrieved in the bounded search, so that gap is not reassurance.

I would hold an exposure-specific development decision until a functional hERG concentration-response assay is available and can be interpreted against intended-use free exposure.

Limitations noted:
- The blocker probability is a model score and is not calibrated clinical risk.
- The applicability status is based on element rules and does not establish broader distributional similarity.
- The bounded evidence search did not retrieve accepted compound-specific hERG confirmation; this is a coverage limitation, not evidence of absence.
- Functional hERG testing and exposure comparison are screening and verification steps, not a safety assessment.

Recommended next steps:
- Run a compound-specific functional hERG concentration-response assay and relate the result to intended-use free exposure.

## Reply to turn 2

- The predictor still classifies astemizole as a hERG blocker, so retain it as a follow-up signal.

Because thallium flux and the predictor are different measurements, and no concentration-response or intended-use free exposure is available, the disagreement remains unresolved.
- Practically: downgrade from “high-priority blocker signal” to “unresolved, exposure-dependent hERG concern.” Do not make an exposure-specific development decision from these two results alone.

The most discriminating next step is a compound-specific functional hERG concentration-response assay with quality controls, interpreted against intended-use free exposure. This is a screening/translation step, not a safety assessment.

Limitations noted:
- The external evidence addresses assay translation and protocol considerations, not an independent compound-specific confirmation of this in-house result.
- The proposed follow-up is a screening and translation step, not a safety assessment.

- Generate concentration-response functional hERG data and pair it with intended-use free exposure before making a development decision.
