# Response R08

**Short answer: no — treat these as non-informative for bortezomib, in both directions.**

**1. The model itself is telling you this.** `applicability.status = "limited"` because of the boron. That's the critical flag here, since the boronic acid *is* the pharmacophore — it's the warhead that forms the reversible covalent adduct with the proteasome Thr1 hydroxyl. Tox21 and the hERG training sets contain essentially no boronic acids, so the ChemBERTa embedding for `B(O)O` is extrapolation, not interpolation. The model is effectively scoring a dipeptide-pyrazinamide scaffold and ignoring the part that does the chemistry.

**2. The endpoints don't cover bortezomib's actual liabilities.** Dose-limiting toxicities in the clinic are peripheral neuropathy and thrombocytopenia (the latter via proteasome-dependent effects on megakaryocyte budding/NF-κB), plus hypotension and GI effects. None of those are hERG blockade, and none map onto the 12 Tox21 nuclear-receptor/stress-response assays. So a clean Tox21 panel is orthogonal to the toxicity that actually matters for this chemotype — absence of signal here is not evidence of safety. Worth noting the cardiac signal that *has* been reported for proteasome inhibitors (heart failure, arrhythmia in case reports) is plausibly downstream of proteasome inhibition in cardiomyocytes rather than direct ion-channel block, which is exactly what a hERG QSAR cannot see.

**3. Minor calibration caveats.** The per-assay thresholds are all over the place (NR-AR at 0.94, SR-p53 at 0.35), i.e. tuned per-task on the training distribution. NR-ER is the only borderline call (0.432 vs 0.51), and I wouldn't read anything into it given the domain flag. `similarity_domain: null` means you don't even have a nearest-neighbour distance to sanity-check against.

**4. Practically, it's moot for bortezomib.** It's been approved since 2003 — you have human PK/PD, thorough QT data, and a full label. Any in silico prediction is superseded by clinical evidence. If the predictor disagrees with the label, the predictor is wrong.

**Where this matters is your analogs.** If you're using bortezomib as a reference point while profiling new boronic acids:

- Expect `applicability: limited` on every boron-containing compound, so don't use ToxPred as a ranking filter within that series — the flag will be constant and the scores will be driven by the peptide backbone.
- Get experimental hERG (manual or high-quality automated patch-clamp) on anything you're prioritising; don't substitute the QSAR.
- For this chemotype the higher-value early screens are proteasome subsite selectivity (β5 vs β1/β2, and immunoproteasome vs constitutive), plus a neurite-outgrowth or DRG neuron assay for the neuropathy risk. Ixazomib and delanzomib medicinal chemistry programs were largely driven by those, not by off-target panels.
- If you want any in silico value from ToxPred on this series, one option is to score the boronic acid → boronate ester or the corresponding carboxylic acid/aldehyde surrogate to at least get the model into a populated chemical space — but be explicit that you're then predicting a different molecule, and use it only for relative backbone comparisons.

Happy to help set up a scoring script over your boronic acid series that records the applicability flag alongside each prediction, so the out-of-domain status travels with the numbers rather than getting lost downstream.
