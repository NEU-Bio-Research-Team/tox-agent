# Response R03

**No.** Several Tox21 activities mean BPA is associated with signals in particular assays; they do not add up to an “overall toxic” classification or show that a given exposure will cause harm. EPA explicitly cautions that activity in one assay does not necessarily imply an adverse health outcome. [EPA ToxCast owner’s manual](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=P101CC2Z.txt)

I would read the results endpoint by endpoint:

- **Check what was measured.** An estrogen receptor agonist result, for example, means activity in that assay’s receptor readout. BPA’s estrogen receptor activity is supported by experimental cell assays, so that signal is biologically plausible. [AroER study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24496634/), [EPA ER bioactivity data](https://www.epa.gov/endocrine-disruption/endocrine-disruptor-screening-program-edsp-estrogen-receptor-bioactivity)
- **Check the concentration and curve quality.** An AC50 describes potency *in that assay*, not a dose that causes harm. Results appearing only near concentrations that impair cell viability may reflect general cell stress rather than a specific target effect. [NIEHS cytotoxicity guidance](https://ntp.niehs.nih.gov/iccvamreport/2023/confidence/assay/28-niehs-cyto-flags)
- **Check whether the model predicted the hits or the assays measured them.** A model score is evidence for prioritizing follow-up, not an independent experimental result or a probability of toxicity.

For a drug discovery decision, I’d prioritize credible, repeatable activities, confirm important hits with a different assay, and compare their active concentrations with plausible **unbound exposure** after accounting for metabolism. The number of positive assays alone is a poor summary of risk.
