# Response R06

**Recommendation:** Hold terfenadine as a lead candidate pending cardiac-safety verification. hERG liability should stop prioritization of the unmodified compound, but does not by itself require abandoning a redesigned analogue programme.

The predictor classifies terfenadine as a hERG blocker. This is supported directionally by external studies placing terfenadine in an intermediate cardiac-risk category and showing that exposure assumptions can move the risk assessment higher.

**Test next:**
1. Run a concentration-response hERG assay for terfenadine with measured free concentrations.
2. Test relevant human metabolites in the same assay and calculate margins against intended and worst-case free exposure.
3. Confirm parent-plus-metabolite effects in an orthogonal human cardiomyocyte or cardiac microphysiological-system assay, measuring action-potential duration and proarrhythmic events.

Advance only if these data show an adequate exposure-to-potency margin with concordant orthogonal cardiac results. Otherwise, deprioritize terfenadine and use the findings to guide analogue design.

Limitations noted:
- The hERG probability is a model score, not a calibrated clinical risk or incidence estimate.
- The proposed assays are screening and verification steps, not a complete safety assessment.
- External evidence supports an intermediate and exposure-sensitive cardiac-risk interpretation, but does not replace compound-specific concentration-response and exposure data.

Recommended next steps:
- Run parent and metabolite hERG concentration-response testing with free-exposure measurements.
- Use an orthogonal human cardiac model to test action-potential and proarrhythmic effects across the relevant exposure range.
