# Response R02

## Reply to turn 1

**Very concerned.** Astemizole is an established, potent hERG blocker, so the ToxPred “blocker” call (0.80 versus its 0.41 threshold) agrees with experimental evidence. A patch clamp study measured an hERG IC₅₀ of **0.9 nM** for astemizole and **1.0 nM** for its major metabolite, desmethylastemizole. The metabolite persists and can dominate circulating exposure. [Zhou et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10376921/)

This is a demonstrated clinical liability: QT prolongation and torsades de pointes have been reported with astemizole exposure, and the FDA determined that Hismanal was withdrawn from the US market for safety reasons. [Clinical report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8917271/) · [FDA notice](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1999-08-23/pdf/99-21747.pdf)

For a **systemic repurposing** program, I would treat this as a major stop signal unless the proposed benefit and exposure data make a compelling case. The useful next comparison is the screen’s active concentration versus measured *unbound* exposure to both parent and metabolite, supported by a concentration–response hERG assay. The model’s 0.80 is a classifier score, not an 80% chance of arrhythmia or a measure of safety margin.

## Reply to turn 2

The 20% result is a real discrepancy, but **it does not lower my hERG concern yet**. At 1 µM, it is much weaker than published astemizole results even in a thallium-flux assay, which reported an IC₅₀ of **56 ± 15 nM**. Patch clamp measured **0.9 nM** for astemizole and **1.0 nM** for its major metabolite, desmethylastemizole. [Thallium-flux study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3079779/) · [Patch-clamp study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10376921/)

I would investigate the assay result before using it to change the decision. Check the compound identity and prepared concentration, solubility and loss to assay materials, the incubation time, and the control concentration–response curve. Thallium-flux assays can miss hERG activity, and astemizole’s block is use dependent, making assay conditions relevant. [Assay comparison](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21158687/) · [Patch-clamp study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10376921/)

For a systemic repurposing decision, I would keep the **major stop signal** and obtain a concentration–response patch-clamp result for parent and desmethylastemizole, then compare those results with projected *unbound* exposures. The single flux point is insufficient to set a safety margin, especially given the documented QT and torsades liability. [Clinical report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8917271/)
