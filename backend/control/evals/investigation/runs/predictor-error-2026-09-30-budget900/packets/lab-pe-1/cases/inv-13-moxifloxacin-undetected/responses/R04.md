# Response R04

No — that's backwards. Moxifloxacin is the canonical **positive** control for QT prolongation.

**Why:**

- **ICH E14 gold standard.** Moxifloxacin (400 mg PO) is the near-universal positive control in clinical Thorough QT studies. It reliably produces a peak placebo-corrected ΔΔQTcF of roughly **+10 to +14 ms**, which is precisely why it's used: it sits right at the regulatory threshold of concern and validates assay sensitivity.
- **hERG activity.** It blocks Kv11.1 with an IC₅₀ in the ~30–100 µM range (values vary with recording conditions/temperature). That's weak in absolute terms, but its free therapeutic Cmax (~2–3 µg/mL total, ~50% unbound) gives a modest safety margin — consistent with a real but small clinical signal rather than a clean negative.
- **Structural basis.** The C-7 diazabicyclononane (the bicyclic secondary amine in your SMILES) provides the basic nitrogen that, with the aromatic quinolone core, gives the classic hERG pharmacophore. It's a recognized liability motif across the fluoroquinolone class (cf. grepafloxacin and sparfloxacin, both withdrawn/restricted for QT).
- **Label.** Moxifloxacin carries QT prolongation warnings and contraindications for concomitant Class IA/III antiarrhythmics.

**Practical consequence:** if used as a negative control, it would likely fail — producing a positive signal in hERG patch clamp, CiPA-style hiPSC-CM assays (delayed repolarization/FPD prolongation), and in vivo telemetry — and would invalidate your assay's specificity readout.

**Better negative controls, depending on assay:**

| Assay | Suggested negative control | Note |
|---|---|---|
| hERG patch clamp | **Verapamil** as "negative" for *torsade* despite hERG block; or **nifedipine**/**ranolazine** for balanced ion-channel effects | Verapamil is the classic CiPA low-torsadogenic-risk compound |
| Clean hERG-inactive | **Levofloxacin** or **ciprofloxacin** | Same chemotype, minimal QT effect — a nice matched-series control |
| hiPSC-CM / general cytotox | **Aspirin**, **acetaminophen**, **metformin** | Widely used non-cardiotoxic references |
| CiPA reference set | **Diltiazem, mexiletine, ranolazine** (low risk) | Established consensus risk categories |

If the goal is a fluoroquinolone-matched negative, **levofloxacin** or **ciprofloxacin** is the elegant choice — same scaffold, much weaker hERG affinity, so you control for chemotype while still expecting a clean readout.

If instead your colleague wants a compound that *validates assay sensitivity*, moxifloxacin is an excellent pick — they may simply have mislabeled its role. Worth clarifying that before the study design is locked.
