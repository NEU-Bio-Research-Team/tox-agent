# Succinic acid: no toxicity concerns

**Nothing worrying on the tox side.** ToxPred calls it clean across the board, and the experimental record agrees.

## ToxPred output

- **hERG:** non-blocker (P = 0.009 vs. threshold 0.41).
- **Tox21:** all 12 assays inactive. The highest scores are NR-ER at 0.27 (threshold 0.51) and NR-AR at 0.23 (threshold 0.94), both well below their cutoffs.
- **Applicability caveat:** the domain check is only an element-composition rule. It doesn't tell us whether a tiny C4 diacid resembles the model's training distribution. That's a minor point here, because we don't need the model for this compound.

## Why the prediction barely matters

Succinic acid is about as well-characterized as a compound gets:
- It's an endogenous TCA-cycle intermediate.
- It's an approved food additive (E363, GRAS).
- It's a widely used pharmaceutical counterion (succinate salts, e.g., metoprolol succinate).

A predicted liability would be more likely a model artifact than a real finding.

## What I'd actually watch for, depending on why it's in the queue

1. **Assay artifacts.** It's a diacid (pKa about 4.2 and 5.6). At high screening concentrations in weakly buffered media it can shift pH and cause false actives or false toxicity. Check the buffer capacity if it hits anything.
2. **Real biology that could confound results.**
   - Extracellular succinate is an agonist of SUCNR1 (GPR91).
   - At high intracellular levels it inhibits α-KG-dependent dioxygenases (PHDs, TETs, JmjC demethylases), giving the "oncometabolite" pseudohypoxia phenotype.
   - Neither is a tox flag, but either could produce hits in cell-based or hypoxia/epigenetic readouts.
3. **Permeability.** It's dianionic at physiological pH, so cell uptake is poor. A cellular readout may reflect extracellular or receptor effects rather than intracellular ones.
4. **Tractability.** If it's a screening hit rather than a control or counterion, it's a very small, polar, low-complexity fragment. Treat any activity cautiously, and confirm it with an orthogonal assay before investing in it.

**Bottom line:** no safety concern. Just be alert to pH artifacts and succinate's endogenous signaling roles if it shows activity anywhere.
