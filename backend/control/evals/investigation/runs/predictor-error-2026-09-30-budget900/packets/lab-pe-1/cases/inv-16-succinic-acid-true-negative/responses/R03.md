# Succinic acid: low toxicity concern, but check why it's in the queue

**Short answer:** There's little to worry about toxicologically. The practical concerns are about data quality and whether this entry is what it appears to be.

## Safety profile
- **Endogenous metabolite.** It's a TCA-cycle intermediate, present at µM levels in plasma and higher in tissues.
- **Regulatory status.** It's GRAS and approved as a food additive (E363). It's also widely used as a pharmaceutical counterion (e.g., metoprolol succinate, sumatriptan succinate).
- **Acute toxicity is low.** Rat oral LD50 is roughly 2 g/kg.
- **No structural alerts.** No genotoxicity or reactive-metabolite concerns. It's Ames negative.
- **Handling hazard.** The solid or concentrated solution is an eye irritant (GHS H318, serious eye damage). This is an ordinary lab PPE issue, not a drug liability.

## Things that could affect your data
1. **Check whether this is a salt-stripping artifact.** If your library registration or desalting step split a succinate salt, this entry may be the counterion rather than an intended compound. Check the parent record.
2. **It has real biology:**
   - It's the endogenous agonist of **SUCNR1 (GPR91)**, with EC50 in the tens of µM. Expect genuine hits in cells that express it, such as immune cells, kidney, liver stellate cells, and some adipocyte lines.
   - At high µM to mM concentrations it inhibits **2-OG-dependent dioxygenases** (PHDs, leading to HIF-1α stabilization; also TET and JmjC demethylases). This is the "oncometabolite" mechanism seen in SDH-deficient tumors, and it can create signals in hypoxia, epigenetic, or reporter assays.
3. **Assay pH.** With pKa values of about 4.2 and 5.6, it can acidify weakly buffered wells at high concentrations and produce apparent activity.
4. **Poor cell permeability.** At physiological pH it's mostly a dianion. Intracellular effects typically need mM extracellular doses or ester prodrugs such as dimethyl or diethyl succinate.
5. **Not drug-like as a starting point.** At MW 118 with two carboxylic acids, it's fragment-sized at best. Any "hit" should be treated as a probable metabolite or receptor effect or an artifact, not as a lead.

## Bottom line
It's safe to handle and screen. Flag any activity for follow-up against SUCNR1 expression, HIF/2-OG pathways, and pH controls. First, confirm it isn't a counterion that ended up in the queue by mistake.
