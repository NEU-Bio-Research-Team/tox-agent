# Response R04

**First thing: this is sotalol.** The SMILES is 4-(1-hydroxy-2-isopropylaminoethyl)methanesulfonanilide — an approved non-selective β-blocker / class III antiarrhythmic. Worth confirming by generating the InChIKey and querying ChEMBL/DrugBank before anything else, but the structure is unambiguous. Approved drugs turning up in screening decks is common, so the first question isn't SAR, it's whether this is a genuine hit on your target, a repurposing lead, or a deck-annotation problem.

**The hERG picture (and why it's an instructive one)**

- Sotalol is a canonical IKr/hERG blocker — it's used as a *positive control* in hERG assays and is a CiPA reference compound in the intermediate/high torsadogenic risk bin. Clinically it produces dose-dependent QT prolongation and has one of the better-documented TdP incidences among marketed drugs.
- But its intrinsic potency is weak: patch-clamp IC50s are typically reported in the high-micromolar range (roughly tens to low hundreds of µM depending on protocol/temperature). By potency alone it looks benign.
- The risk comes entirely from **margin, not potency**: it's dosed at high mg levels, has essentially negligible plasma protein binding, and is cleared renally as unchanged drug. Free plasma concentrations reach the tens of µM, so free Cmax sits close to the hERG IC50. Renal impairment or accumulation collapses the margin further.
- Practical consequence for your workflow: most ligand-based hERG QSAR models will score this compound as low risk. The standard hERG pharmacophore wants a basic amine plus two or three lipophilic aromatic/hydrophobic groups and logP > ~3; sotalol is hydrophilic (logP ≈ −0.4) with a single ring. It's a well-known **false negative** for in silico hERG filters. If your triage pipeline flagged it green, that's a calibration signal about the model, not reassurance about the molecule.

**What I'd do next**

1. *Confirm identity and provenance* — InChIKey match against approved-drug sets; check whether the hit is reproducible and whether the deck well is what the annotation claims (re-test from fresh powder, LC-MS confirmation).
2. *Decide the question you're actually asking.* If sotalol itself is the interesting molecule (repurposing), the cardiac liability is already fully characterized in the clinical literature and the work is PK/dose/patient-selection, not med chem. If it's a scaffold starting point, proceed to 3.
3. *Deconvolute the pharmacology* — run a β1/β2/α-adrenergic panel. The aryl-ethanolamine-isopropylamine motif is a β-adrenergic pharmacophore; unless that's your target, this is an on-scaffold off-target you'll have to engineer out, and it will co-vary with your SAR.
4. *Get proper electrophysiology on any follow-ups* — manual or high-quality automated patch clamp rather than binding/flux displacement, with attention to state dependence and temperature. Add Cav1.2 and late Nav1.5 so you can run a CiPA-style multi-channel in-silico proarrhythmia assessment; net current effects matter more than hERG in isolation.
5. *Add a functional readout* — hiPSC-CM field potential/impedance or optical mapping for repolarization prolongation and early-afterdepolarization/arrhythmia proxies.
6. *Set the margin criterion early and use free drug* — I'd anchor on free-Cmax-corrected hERG margin (commonly ≥30×) and force the team to include projected human dose and plasma protein binding in the calculation. Sotalol is the textbook case where ignoring free fraction and dose would have produced the wrong conclusion.
7. *If hERG becomes a real issue in the series*, the usual levers are reducing amine basicity (pKa), trimming logP, introducing an acidic or zwitterionic element, and disrupting the aromatic/amine geometry — but recognize that for a sotalol-like chemotype the more productive lever is often PK (lower dose, higher protein binding, non-renal clearance) rather than intrinsic potency.
