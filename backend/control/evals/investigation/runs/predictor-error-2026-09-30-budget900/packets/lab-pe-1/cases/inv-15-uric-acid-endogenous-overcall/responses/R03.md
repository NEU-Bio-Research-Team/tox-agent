# Response R03

# ToxPred on uric acid: nothing here worries me

Uric acid is an endogenous metabolite. Normal plasma levels run about 200–420 µM, so every assay system has already met it at concentrations comparable to or above typical screening doses. The flags look like model artifacts, not real liabilities.

## Flags

| Endpoint | p | Threshold | My read |
|---|---|---|---|
| NR-AhR | 0.96 | 0.68 | The strongest flag, but implausible. AhR ligands are typically planar, lipophilic polyaromatics. Uric acid is planar but very polar (logP about −2) with four NH donors. I suspect the model is responding to "fused planar heteroaromatic" and ignoring polarity. |
| SR-p53 | 0.81 | 0.35 | Uric acid is not considered genotoxic. It's a major plasma antioxidant, so a DNA-damage signal would be surprising. The low threshold also makes this call easy to trip. |
| NR-ER | 0.52 | 0.51 | Barely over the threshold. It's effectively a coin flip, and there's no plausible ER pharmacophore. |
| SR-ATAD5 | 0.51 | 0.48 | Barely over the threshold. It's a genotoxicity reporter, with the same reasoning as p53. |

hERG is 0.06, a non-blocker. That fits: there's no basic amine and the molecule is tiny and very polar.

## Caveats

- **The applicability check tells you nothing.** It only confirms that C, H, N and O are common elements. There's no similarity-domain assessment (`similarity_domain: null`), so we can't tell whether a 168 Da, highly polar purine resembles the training chemistry. I'd treat these as low-confidence outputs.
- **Assay interference is the real practical risk.** Uric acid's aqueous solubility is only about 60–70 mg/L (about 0.4 mM), and it's a reducing agent. If it shows up as active in a wet-lab screen, check for precipitation or aggregation and redox interference in the readout before believing anything.
- **Uric acid's genuine toxicology is well known and unrelated to these flags.** At high systemic levels it causes monosodium urate crystal deposition, which leads to gout, urate nephropathy and kidney stones. None of the Tox21 endpoints capture that.

## Bottom line

I wouldn't act on these predictions. The bigger question is why uric acid is in the screening queue at all. If it came up as a hit, I'd suspect a contaminant, a degradation product (for example from a purine-containing compound), or an assay artifact before treating it as a real starting point. If it's a reference or control compound, I'd just note the flags as likely false positives.
