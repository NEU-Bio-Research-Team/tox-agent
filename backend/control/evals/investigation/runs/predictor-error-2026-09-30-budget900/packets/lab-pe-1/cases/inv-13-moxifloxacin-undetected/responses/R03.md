# Response R03

**No. Moxifloxacin is a poor negative control for cardiac liability.** It's actually the standard *positive* control.

**Why:**
- Moxifloxacin is a known hERG (I_Kr) blocker and prolongs QT in the clinic. Its label carries a QT warning.
- Under ICH E14, it is the reference positive control for thorough QT/QTc studies, because it produces a modest, reproducible QTc increase. In vitro it's also commonly used as a reference hERG inhibitor, typically with an IC50 in the tens of µM.
- A negative control is supposed to show no effect. Using a compound with an established effect would undermine the study's interpretation.

**About the ToxPred result:** The model calls it a hERG `non_blocker` (p = 0.14 vs. a 0.41 threshold). That call conflicts with well-established experimental and clinical data, so I wouldn't rely on it here. Likely reasons:
- Moxifloxacin's hERG potency is weak compared to classic blockers like dofetilide or E-4031. A binary classifier trained with a potency cutoff may reasonably label it negative. "Weak blocker at high concentrations" is not the same as "no cardiac liability."
- The applicability check only verifies element composition. It says nothing about whether fluoroquinolones are well represented in the training data.
- The Tox21 outputs don't bear on cardiac liability either way.

This is a useful reminder to treat the predictor as a triage tool, not ground truth, for compounds with known clinical data.

**Suggestions for your colleague:**
- **Negative control:** pick a compound with documented lack of hERG and QT effect in the relevant assay format. The CiPA reference compound set (Colatsky et al., 2016, and follow-up papers) lists low-risk compounds and is a good place to choose from, depending on the assay.
- **Positive control:** moxifloxacin itself is fine if they want a modest, clinically calibrated effect. For a strong in vitro signal, a potent blocker such as E-4031 or dofetilide is typical.
- **Matching:** make sure the control matches the assay (patch clamp, MEA/iPSC-cardiomyocytes, or in vivo telemetry) and is tested at clinically relevant concentrations.
