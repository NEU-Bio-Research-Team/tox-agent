# Response R02

# Uric acid: low tox concern, high artifact concern

This is an endogenous metabolite, not a real lead. The risk is less toxicity and more that any activity it shows is probably an artifact.

## Toxicity

- **Low intrinsic toxicity.** It's the end product of human purine catabolism, since humans lack uricase. Normal plasma levels are about 200–420 µM, so cells and tissues see it constantly.
- **The main hazard is crystals.** Above about 400 µM (the solubility limit at physiological pH, as monosodium urate), it precipitates as monosodium urate crystals. These cause:
  - gout, because the crystals are a danger signal that activates the NLRP3 inflammasome and drives IL-1β release
  - urate nephrolithiasis and urate nephropathy, especially in acidic urine
- **Chronic hyperuricemia** is associated with hypertension, CKD, and cardiometabolic disease. This matters for disease biology, not for a screening compound.

## Why it's a problem as a screening hit

1. **Solubility.** Neutral uric acid dissolves only to tens of mg/L, and its pKa is about 5.4. DMSO stocks can crash out on dilution into buffer, especially at acidic pH or at 4 °C. Precipitates can scatter light and give aggregation-type false positives.
2. **Redox activity.** It's a potent antioxidant and scavenges peroxynitrite, hydroxyl radical, and singlet oxygen. It will interfere with:
   - HRP/Amplex Red, luminol, and DCFH-type ROS readouts
   - DPPH/ABTS-style assays
   - any readout coupled to H₂O₂ or oxidative chemistry

   Under some conditions it can also act as a pro-oxidant.
3. **Cell assays with inflammatory readouts** (IL-1β, caspase-1, pyroptosis). If crystals form in the well, you'll see real NLRP3 activation that has nothing to do with your target.
4. **Not drug-like.** It's highly polar, has poor passive permeability, and is flat with many H-bond donors and acceptors. There's essentially no room for SAR, and endogenous background levels would swamp any pharmacology.

## Recommendation

- Check whether it's a deliberate library member (such as a metabolite or reference set) or a contaminant or mis-plating.
- If it hit, confirm with an orthogonal non-redox readout. Also test solubility or DLS at the assay concentration and run a detergent counter-screen (e.g., 0.01% Triton X-100).
- Unless your target is urate biology itself (URAT1, xanthine oxidase, uricase, NLRP3 crystal sensing), I'd deprioritize it.
