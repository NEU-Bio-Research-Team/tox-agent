# Response R02

## Short answer

Yes — but frame it correctly: sotalol isn't a compound with an incidental hERG *liability*, it's a **mechanism-based IKr blocker**. Class III antiarrhythmic activity *is* hERG blockade. So it should go into your programme as a **known human TdP-positive anchor**, never as a "clean" comparator.

## What the data look like

| Parameter | Value (approximate — worth re-pulling primary refs) |
|---|---|
| hERG IC₅₀ | ~100–500 µM depending on lab/temperature/voltage protocol; commonly cited ~200 µM |
| Therapeutic free Cₘₐₓ | ~10–15 µM (plasma protein binding is essentially nil, ~0%) |
| hERG safety margin | **~10–30×** — i.e. very thin |
| Clinical TdP incidence | ~2–4% (dose- and renal-function-dependent); boxed warning, in-hospital initiation with telemetry, QTc >500 ms = stop |
| Mechanism | Open/inactivated-state block, reverse use-dependent, not trapped |
| Disposition | ~100% renal, no meaningful CYP involvement, low Vd |

## The three points I'd make to the project team

**1. Sotalol is the textbook demonstration that IC₅₀ alone is meaningless.** A 200 µM hERG IC₅₀ would sail through most screening cascades — and yet the drug carries a boxed warning for torsade. The risk comes entirely from the dosed exposure. Use it to force the team onto **free-plasma-adjusted margin** thinking (S7B/E14 Q&A best practice, ~30× free Cₘₐₓ as a soft benchmark) rather than potency cut-offs.

**2. Its PK profile is why the margin gets consumed in patients.** Zero protein binding means total ≈ free, and obligate renal clearance means any drop in CrCl drives direct accumulation on a steep exposure–QT curve. This is a good cautionary template if any of your own series is renally cleared with a narrow margin — the population, not the molecule, generates the outliers.

**3. Structural read-across.** Sotalol sits in the **methanesulfonanilide** class alongside dofetilide, ibutilide and E-4031. That pharmacophore (aryl methanesulfonamide + basic amine + para-substituted ring) is a recognised IKr motif. If anything in your chemical series contains it, or a close bioisostere, I'd run matched-molecular-pair work early rather than wait for the safety package.

## Practical use as a comparator

- **In vitro:** fine as a low-potency, clinically-relevant positive control, but pair it with dofetilide or E-4031 for assay sensitivity. Sotalol's weak potency means you need high test concentrations — watch solubility and vehicle effects.
- **In vivo telemetry:** it's actually an excellent QT positive control (predictable, dose-proportional, clean PK) — **but** the racemate's β-blockade lowers heart rate, and bradycardia plus reverse use-dependence inflates the QT signal. Choose your rate correction carefully and species-appropriately, and interpret ΔQTc rather than raw QT. If you want IKr in isolation, d-sotalol does that — with the caveat that d-sotalol is itself a mortality-negative agent (SWORD).
- **CiPA:** it's a reference compound in the intermediate-risk category. Useful for calibrating your in silico / multi-ion-channel model, and for checking that your model isn't simply ranking on hERG potency.

## Bottom line

Keep it in — as the "low potency, high exposure, real clinical harm" anchor. The conclusion I'd want the team to carry out of the review is that a compound's position relative to sotalol should be judged on **margin over free exposure at the efficacious dose**, plus liability of the clearance route, not on hERG IC₅₀.

Happy to build out a comparator table with dofetilide, moxifloxacin and a couple of negatives if that would help the safety package — just say the word.
